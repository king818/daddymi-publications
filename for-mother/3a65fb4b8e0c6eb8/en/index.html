For Duanduan's Mother

This song is sung to you by Duanduan and family, together. The gratitude that is so hard to say out loud is all here, in these few minutes — thank you for a lifetime of meals; thank you for folding your love, spoon by spoon, into every bowl of lei cha and every dinner table.

May you sleep peacefully at night — as peacefully as you once sang us to sleep.

May the pain in your body slowly fade, and your strength return, a little more each day.

May there always be light in your heart — like the lamp in that old hometown kitchen: warm, and softly bright.

May you never have to worry about anything again: the meals will be cooked, the lamp will be kept, and the family — the family has us now.

All you need to do is rest easy, eat well, and sleep well.

We are right here beside you. Always.

The Story Behind This Song

It begins in a hometown kitchen. Until Duanduan was fourteen, every meal in that house was cooked on an old-fashioned stove — firewood crackling, white steam rising from the iron wok, the stove’s warmth filling the whole morning. Mother stood at that stove for half a lifetime: porridge at dawn, lunch at noon, a generous dinner every night — and she was always the last one to sit down.

Under the lamp is the sight of her making lei cha. Peanuts, sesame and tea leaves go into the mortar, ground round and round, slowly, until the fragrance rises; the tea is mixed with rice and served with a plate of greens. That is this family’s own taste — the sound of a mother grinding her love into the days.

Later, the family moved to Tianhe, Guangzhou. The kitchen changed; the old stove gave way to gas. But Mother’s cooking never changed, not for a single day. Now the daughter is grown, and there is one sentence she has kept in her heart for a long time:

“Don’t worry. I’ve grown up. This family has me now — lean on me.”
