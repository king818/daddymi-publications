He arrived with empty pockets — and a plan he had made at 14.

His story

Arnold Schwarzenegger grew up in Thal, a small village in Austria. At 14 he made a plan: while no one else could see it yet, he already saw his future clearly. First came bodybuilding — he became Mr. Universe — and in 1968 he arrived in America with empty pockets and one sentence in his heart: it can be done.

Then came Hollywood, and then the Governor’s office in California. His story also has a chapter of falling — and of taking responsibility, receiving mercy, and beginning again. Through it all he insists on one thing: never call him "self-made" — teachers, training partners and friends carried him along the way. The summary he writes over his own life is: Be useful — turn your strength outward, toward other people.

In Arnold’s own words

"The first rule of success is to have a vision. You see, if you don’t have a vision of where you’re going, if you don’t have a goal where you go, you drift around and you never end up anywhere."

"So whenever someone said to me, it can’t be done, I heard it can be done. And they said no, I heard yes. And when they said it’s impossible, I heard it is possible."

"Don’t be afraid of failing. Because there’s nothing wrong with failing. You have to fail in order to climb that ladder. There’s no one that doesn’t fail. What is not okay is that when you fail, you stay down. Whoever stays down is a loser. And winners will fail and get up, fail and get up, fail and get up. You always get up. That is a winner; that is a winner."

"You can call me anything you want; you can call me Arnie, you can call me Schwarzy, you can call me Terminator, you can call me Governator, but don’t ever call me self-made man. I said because I did not get to that point by myself."

— Arnold Schwarzenegger, as quoted in the film
