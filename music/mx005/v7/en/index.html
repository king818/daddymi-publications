Lei Cha Beneath the Lamp

Thank You, Mom
Mi Xi

A mother who loved her family with a lifetime of meals — from the morning stove to the abundant dinner table every night; and in that bowl of lei cha, ground with her own hands, hid a love she never said aloud.
Now she is ill, and her children keep watch at her bedside, wanting only to tell her in person: Thank you, Mom. Everything you did — we saw it all, and we remember. Don't worry; I am here for this family now.

Making-of Notes

This song was written for a mother who is ill. Her story is true: she loved her family with a lifetime of meals — three hundred sixty-five days a year, from the morning stove to the abundant dinner table every night, without a break; in that bowl of lei cha she ground with her own hands, peanuts and sesame were slowly ground fragrant; only after everyone at the full table had finished did she sit down, last of all, to eat.

Now she is ill. Her story has been told again and again, until the one telling it is the first to tear up — the story came first; then came this song.

Take 7 attacks one problem only: the opening. Its grandest orchestral passage was moved to the very front — the Prelude version lands at full weight from the first beat, an opening 2.8 times the weight of Take 1, and the only one of the three to pass the opening test. The other two are fresh generations whose openings are still light; all three are presented honestly for your comparison.
